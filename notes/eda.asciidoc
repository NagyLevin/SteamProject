= Exploratory Data Analysis


After completing data cleaning and user-level
feature engineering, I performed exploratory
data analysis (EDA) on the resulting dataset.

The purpose of EDA is to understand the
distribution and variability of the behavioral
features before applying clustering algorithms.

The analysis focuses on identifying differences
in game ownership, recorded playtime, and
player engagement.

The dataset contains 12,393 user observations
and six numerical behavioral features.

I excluded the `user_id` column from the
statistical analysis because it is an identifier
rather than a behavioral feature.

I used the Pandas `describe()` method to calculate
descriptive statistics for each numerical feature.

The `.T` attribute was used to transpose the
resulting table for easier comparison.

|===

|games_played |5.69 |17.08 |1 |1 |3 |498
|total_playtime |278.01 |760.27 |1.3 |13.5 |155.6 |11754

|max_playtime |176.16 |504.76 |1.2 |11.6 |107 |11754
|play_ratio |0.727 |0.345 |0.5 |1.0 |1.0 |1.0

The descriptive statistics suggest that user
behavior is highly unevenly distributed.
The average number of purchased games is 10.39,
while the median is only 2. This suggests that
most users have relatively small recorded game
libraries, while a smaller number of users own
significantly more games.
A similar pattern can be observed in total
playtime. The average recorded playtime is
278.01 hours, compared to a median of only
13.5 hours.
The large differences between the means and
medians, together with high standard deviations,
indicate strongly right-skewed distributions.
The maximum recorded library size is 1,068 games,
and the maximum total playtime is 11,754 hours.

I used the Pandas `quantile()` method to calculate
the 90th, 95th, and 99th percentiles of the
`games_purchased` feature.

The following results were obtained:

* 90th percentile: 20 games
* 95th percentile: 42 games
* 99th percentile: 154.16 games

The results indicate that approximately 90% of
users own no more than 20 games, while approximately
95% own no more than 42 games.

The 99th percentile is approximately 154 games,
compared to the maximum recorded value of
1,068 games.

A relatively small proportion of users have
substantially larger game libraries than the
majority of the dataset.

== Games Purchased Distribution Visualization

To better understand the distribution of game
ownership, I divided users into several categories
based on their number of purchased games.

The categories include individual values for
users owning 1, 2, or 3 games, followed by
larger intervals for users with bigger libraries.
I used the Pandas `pd.cut()` function to assign
each user to a category.

The `value_counts()` method was then used to
calculate the number of users in each group.

A horizontal bar chart was created using Matplotlib.

The distribution analysis was performed on
12,393 Steam users.

The results showed that 5,700 users (46.0%)
owned exactly one game.

A total of 8,003 users (64.6%) owned no more
than three games, while 11,185 users (90.3%)
owned no more than 20 games.

Only 245 users (2.0%) owned more than 100 games.

These findings confirm that the distribution
of purchased games is strongly right-skewed.

== Total Playtime Distribution

To investigate differences in gaming activity,
I analyzed the distribution of the
`total_playtime` feature.

Users were divided into categories based on
their total recorded playtime, ranging from
zero hours to more than 1,000 hours.

This analysis aims to identify differences
in recorded player activity and investigate
the previously observed right-skewed
distribution of total playtime.

The distribution analysis included all 12,393 users.

The results showed that 1,043 users (8.4%) had
zero recorded playtime.

A total of 5,814 users (46.9%) had no more
than 10 hours of recorded playtime.

Furthermore, 8,776 users (70.8%) had no more
than 100 hours of recorded playtime.

However, 1,020 users (8.2%) had more than
1,000 hours of recorded playtime.

These findings indicate that total playtime
is strongly right-skewed.

A large proportion of users have relatively
low recorded playtime, while a smaller group
shows substantially higher gaming activity.

This pattern is consistent with the descriptive
statistics, where the mean total playtime was
278.01 hours, compared to a median of 13.5 hours.

== Implications for Clustering

The substantial differences in recorded playtime
suggest that this feature may be useful for
distinguishing different levels of player activity.

== Relationship Between Game Ownership and Playtime

To investigate the relationship between game
ownership and recorded playtime, I grouped users
based on the number of games they purchased.

I used the Pandas `pd.cut()` function to divide
users into nine game ownership categories.

Then, I applied `groupby()` and `agg()` to calculate
the number of users, mean total playtime, and
median total playtime for each category.

== Correlation Analysis
The purpose of correlation analysis is to
investigate the relationships between the six
numerical behavioral features.

Understanding these relationships is important
because strongly correlated features may
represent overlapping aspects of user behavior.

This can influence the results of distance-based
clustering algorithms such as K-Means.

I excluded the `user_id` column because it
represents a user identifier rather than
a behavioral characteristic.

I used the Pandas `corr()` method to calculate
a correlation matrix containing all six
behavioral features.

The Spearman correlation method was selected
because the earlier exploratory analysis
identified strongly right-skewed distributions
and extreme values.

Spearman correlation measures the strength and
direction of monotonic relationships based on
ranked observations.

Its values range from -1 to +1.

A value close to +1 indicates a strong positive
monotonic relationship, while a value close to
-1 indicates a strong negative monotonic
relationship.

Values close to zero indicate a weak monotonic
relationship.

=== Correlation Analysis Results

The Spearman correlation analysis revealed several
strong relationships between the behavioral features.

The strongest positive correlation was observed
between `total_playtime` and `max_playtime` (0.99).

Similarly, `avg_playtime` showed strong positive
correlations with both `max_playtime` (0.97) and
`total_playtime` (0.94).

These results suggest that the three playtime-related
features contain substantially overlapping information.

One possible explanation is that many users
concentrate their recorded playtime on a small
number of games.

The number of purchased and played games also
showed a strong positive correlation (0.76).

Furthermore, `games_played` and `total_playtime`
had a positive correlation of 0.72, suggesting
that users who play more distinct games generally
have higher total recorded playtime.

A negative correlation of -0.60 was observed
between `games_purchased` and `play_ratio`.

This suggests that users with larger recorded
game libraries tend to have a lower proportion
of games with recorded playtime.

The high correlations between the playtime-related
features suggest potential redundancy.

Using several strongly related features may
increase the influence of similar behavioral
information in distance-based clustering.

Therefore, different feature combinations should
be considered during the later clustering stage.

For example, a model using all six features
could be compared against a model using a
reduced feature set.

== Outlier Analysis

I performed an outlier analysis to
identify unusually high or low observations.

I used the Interquartile Range (IQR) method
to identify potential outliers.

The IQR was calculated as the difference
between the third quartile (Q3) and the first
quartile (Q1).

The lower and upper boundaries were defined as:

* Lower bound = Q1 - 1.5 * IQR
* Upper bound = Q3 + 1.5 * IQR

I applied this method to the `games_purchased`
and `total_playtime` features.

Extremely high values do not necessarily
represent incorrect data.

Users with large game libraries or extensive
recorded playtime may represent legitimate
behavioral patterns relevant to clustering.

After running the outlier analysis i got the following resoults:

[cols="2,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Feature |Upper Bound |Outlier Count |Outlier Percentage

|games_purchased |13.50 |1672 |13.49%
|total_playtime |387.05 |2072 |16.72%
|===

For `games_purchased`, the first quartile was 1
and the third quartile was 6, resulting in an
IQR of 5.

The upper outlier boundary was 13.5 games.

A total of 1,672 users (13.49%) owned at least
14 games and were identified as potential outliers.

For `total_playtime`, the first quartile was
1.3 hours and the third quartile was 155.6 hours.

The resulting IQR was 154.3 hours, and the upper
outlier boundary was 387.05 hours.

A total of 2,072 users (16.72%) exceeded this
boundary.

The results confirm that the distributions of
game ownership and recorded playtime contain
a substantial number of unusually high values.

However, these observations do not necessarily
represent incorrect data.

For example, users owning more than 13 games
may be considered outliers by the IQR method
because the majority of users in the dataset
have very small recorded game libraries.

Similarly, users with more than 387.05 hours
of recorded playtime may represent legitimate
highly active players.

Removing these users could exclude meaningful
behavioral patterns from the clustering analysis.

Therefore i decided to keep all user observations.

== EDA Conclusions

The exploratory data analysis was performed on
12,393 Steam users using six numerical behavioral
features.

The analysis revealed several important patterns
in game ownership and recorded playtime behavior.

First, game ownership was highly unevenly
distributed. Approximately 46.0% of users owned
only one game, while 90.3% owned no more than
20 games.

The mean number of purchased games was 10.39,
compared to a median of only 2.

Second, total recorded playtime was also strongly
right-skewed.

Approximately 70.8% of users had no more than
100 hours of recorded playtime, while 8.2% had
more than 1,000 hours.

The mean total playtime was 278.01 hours,
compared to a median of 13.5 hours.

Third, users with larger game libraries generally
showed higher recorded total playtime.

For example, users owning only one game had a
median total playtime of 2.70 hours, compared
to 1,860.50 hours among users owning more
than 100 games.

Fourth, the correlation analysis identified
strong relationships between several features.

In particular, `total_playtime` and
`max_playtime` showed a Spearman correlation
of 0.99, suggesting potentially overlapping
information.

Finally, the IQR-based outlier analysis
identified 1,672 users (13.49%) with unusually
high numbers of purchased games and 2,072
users (16.72%) with unusually high total
recorded playtime.

These potential outliers were retained because
they may represent meaningful gaming behavior
rather than incorrect observations.
